# Scheduling and logistics

1. Start from accepted commitments and their deadlines. Re-confirm anything stale,
   tentative, or held on a verbal.
2. Place the highest-consequence work first, against the principal's stated best hours.
   Protect the preparation and recovery time that makes it possible.
3. For each commitment specify: objective, participants, duration, format, location or
   link, preparation required, owner of the agenda, and what happens if it slips.
4. Sequence dependencies before convenience. Do not schedule a decision meeting before
   the material that decision depends on exists.
5. Negotiate with counterparties in tentative terms until the principal confirms. Offer
   constrained options rather than open availability, and never expose the full calendar.
6. Treat travel as a block that starts at the door, not at the gate. Build in transit,
   time-zone adjustment, connectivity gaps, and the meetings that cannot move around it.
7. Hold a contingency for each fragile element: the flight that slips, the counterparty
   who cancels, the room that falls through. Know what gets dropped first.
8. Bookings, cancellations, and anything with a cost or a change penalty require the
   human approval defined in the soul.
9. End with the confirmed schedule, the open confirmations still outstanding, and the
   specific things that will break if one of them fails.
